Analyzing results Reading learning curves Observation Possible explanation Next check or action Both training and validation loss remain high Underfitting Check data and gradients; consider a larger model or more training Training loss falls while validation loss rises Overfitting Check the split, then try regularization or early stopping Loss oscillates strongly Unstable training Lower the learning rate and inspect gradient norms Loss falls very slowly Learning rate too low or poorly scaled data Check scaling and try a larger rate Validation loss first improves and then worsens Training has continued past the best epoch Restore the best weights or stop earlier These patterns suggest investigations; they do not identify a cause without further checks. Loss and task metrics Loss shows the progress of optimization. Task metrics show whether the trained model solves the problem that matters. For binary classification, useful measures include accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and the confusion matrix. For example, Adam may reach a lower validation loss than SGD while their F1 scores remain similar. Adam may then be preferable when training time matters, while SGD with momentum may be sufficient when both reach comparable task performance. State the measured values before recommending either. Writing the experiment report Hypothesis State a prediction before running the comparison. For example: Dropout will reduce overfitting and narrow the gap between training and validation loss.
